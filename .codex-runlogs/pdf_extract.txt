E-Gate: An Electronic Event Registration and  
QR-Enabled Pass System 
 
 
 
OVERVIEW 
The Barangay 663 -A Identification System is a digital 
identification and event -attendance platform that enables 
barangay officials to efficiently register residents, issue secure ID 
cards, and manage participation in community programs. The 
system replaces manual paper-based processes with a QR-enabled 
verification workflow and an optional facial recognition feature 
for enhanced security. 
 
Residents can create digital accounts, update their profiles, and 
access their virtual barangay ID, which includes a QR code linked 
to their unique Barangay ID. Barangay officials utilize a web -
based administrator portal to manage community events, track 
registrants, monitor real-time attendance, and export participation 
reports. 
 
The backend is developed using Django and Django REST 
Framework with JWT authentication, while the frontend is built 
with React and Vite. QR scanning is supported through HTML5-
Qrcode and WebRTC for camera access. When enabled, facial 
recognition uses face  embeddings to match live images against 
stored resident templates. 
 
This project aligns with the ongoing digital transformation efforts 
in local government units and with SDG 9: Industry, Innovation, 
and Infrastructure, promoting efficient and secure public service 
delivery through technology. 
 
In addition to its core features, the system also supports gate 
operations and guest appointment management. It allows real -
time logging of resident time -in and time -out, event attendance 
validation, and guest check -in and check -out processes. The 
platform is designed not only for event management but also as a 
broader barangay operations system that improves efficiency, 
organization, and data tracking across multiple service

Background of the Study 
Barangays function as the smallest administrative units in the 
Philippines, responsible for frontline service delivery. Many 
barangays still depend on manual encoding and physical 
documents to manage residents and event participants. This leads 
to errors, inefficiencies, and accessibility issues, especially during 
events that require strict eligibility or limited resources [3]. 
Inefficient verification systems may result in unauthorized 
attendance, repeated claiming of benefits, and inaccurate 
reporting, which compromises fairness and transparency [2]. 
 
Existing commercial event platforms focus on public ticketing 
and payment-based events, making them unsuitable for barangay 
operations because they lack resident -specific verification, ID 
management, and QR - based identity validation [5]. The 
Barangay 663 -A Identification System addresses these gaps by 
tailoring its workflow to barangay needs —resident registration, 
barangay ID generation, controlled event registration, and secure 
QR/face recognition-based attendance. This aligns with ongoing 
digital transfo rmation efforts in both local government and 
educational institutions, where digitization enhances efficiency 
and minimizes operational risks [4], [6]. 
 
Purpose and Description 
The purpose of this study is to design and develop a web - based 
identification and event attendance system that securely registers 
residents, generates QR -enabled barangay IDs, and streamlines 
participation in barangay programs through digital validation 
mechanisms. The system replaces manual paperwork with a 
centralized, digital, and verifiable workflow supported by modern 
authentication and data processing approaches [7]. 
 
Key features such as QR scanning and optional facial recognition 
draw from established studies highlighting the importance of 
biometric verification and real -time authentication in preventing 
identity fraud and improving operational accuracy [1], [6]. 
 
Objectives of the Study

To develop a secure web -based identification and event 
attendance system that automates resident verification, enhances 
event management, and supports real -time monitoring for 
Barangay 663-A.

Scope and Limitations 
 
Scope 
The system includes digital resident registration, QR -based ID 
generation, event setup, registration management, real - time 
attendance verification, optional facial recognition, and 
attendance analytics. These features align with studies 
emphasizing digita l transformation for accurate, secure, and 
accessible public service delivery. The system also includes gate 
operation features such as resident logging and guest appointment 
processing, as well as data archival for residents, events, and guest 
records. 
 
Limitations 
Facial recognition is optional and may be used as an additional 
layer of verification; however, its accuracy may vary under 
inconsistent lighting conditions or significant facial changes. 
Internet connectivity is required for real-time QR and verification 
processes. The system does not integrate with national 
government databases. Existing literature notes similar 
limitations in real-time access control systems used in developing 
communities. 
Definition of Terms 
Electronic Registration: A digital process wherein residents are 
registered into the barangay system through an online or 
computer-based form, allowing their personal information to be 
stored, updated, and managed in a centralized database. 
Barangay Identification System: A digital platform used by the 
barangay to create, manage, and verify resident records, serving 
as the official basis for issuing digital IDs and validating 
eligibility for barangay events and services. 
Virtual Barangay ID: A digital version of a resident’s barangay 
identification card, displayed on a screen or printed, that includes 
the resident’s details, photo, and QR code linked to their unique 
Barangay ID in the system. 
QR-Enabled Pass:  A digital credential, typically shown on a 
mobile device or printed as part of the virtual barangay ID, 
containing a QR code that can be scanned to verify a resident’s 

===== PAGE 3 =====
identity and registration for a barangay event or service. 
Real-Time Validation: The instantaneous process of checking a 
scanned QR code or captured image against the central barangay 
database at the moment of entry, allowing immediate 
confirmation or rejection of a resident’s eligibility. 
Facial Recognition Verification:  A biometric technology that 
confirms a resident’s identity by comparing a live facial image 
captured from the webcam with stored facial data in the system. 
In the Barangay 663 -A Identification System, it is used as an 
optional additional verification step alongside QR scanning. 
QR Code: A scannable code linked to a resident or guest record 
within the system database for identification and verification 
purposes. 
JSON Web Token (JWT):  A compact, digitally signed token 
used for securely transmitting user identity and authorization data 
between the frontend and backend. In this system, JWTs are 
issued upon login and must be presented to access protected API 
endpoints. 
Resident Profile:  A structured digital record in the database 
containing a resident’s personal details such as name, address, 
birthdate, Barangay ID, profile photo, QR code, and optional 
facial data if enabled. 
Web-Based Scanner:  A browser -based interface that uses the 
device camera, via technologies like HTML5 and WebRTC, to 
scan QR codes or capture images for identity verification and 
attendance marking during barangay events or services. 
Event Attendance System: The module of the Barangay 663 -A 
Identification System responsible for managing event 
registrations, validating participants through QR and optional 
facial recognition, and recording attendance data for reporting and 
analytics. 
Gate Portal:  A system interface used by personnel to monitor 
and validate entries in real time, including resident logging and 
event attendance. 
Guest Appointment: A scheduled visit recorded in the system 
with corresponding QR-based validation and tracking. 
Time-In/Time-Out Logging: A feature that records the entry and 
exit of residents within the barangay for monitoring and tracking 
purposes.

Software Development Life Cycle 
The project adopted the Agile–Scrum Model, an iterative SDLC 
approach known for promoting flexibility and continuous 
feedback. Literature emphasizes Scrum’s effectiveness in 
developing systems with evolving requirements and multiple 
stakeholders. 
 
For the development of the E -Gate system, the proponents 
selected the Agile Model, specifically the Scrum Framework. 
This choice was driven by its adaptive and iterative nature, which 
is ideal for a project with evolving requirements and a need for 
continuous stakeholder feedback. Unlike traditional, rigid 
models, Scrum allows the team to focus on developing high -
priority features in short, manageable cycles, enabling them to 
quickly incorporate changes and identify issues early in the 
development process [ 30]. The iterative testing inherent in each 
cycle ensures a higher quality product and reduces the risk of 
major failures.

The development of E-Gate followed the structured phases of the 
Scrum framework: 
 
Requirement Gathering: The team conducted initial meetings

sprints. 
Deployment: After multiple sprints resulted in a stable and 
feature-complete system, it was deployed to a production

improvements.

secure verification at every checkpoint.

individuals can use digital passes —discouraging pass sharing, 
forgery, and unauthorized access. Ultimately, the E-Gate System 
promotes transparency, improves service delivery efficiency, 
secures sensitive dat a, and supports the barangay’s transition 
toward modern, technology-driven public service management.

Security of the Proposed System 
 
The E-Gate system implements comprehensive security measures using the implemented technologies: 
 
JWT Authentication with Simple JWT The system uses Django Simple JWT for secure token -based authentication. When 
administrators log in with their credentials, the Django backend validates them and returns an access token and refresh token . This token 
must be 
 
included in the header of every subsequent request to access protected API endpoints. The tokens are digitally signed using Django's security 
settings and have a configurable lifespan, reducing the risk of unauthorized access. 
 
Additional Security Measures:
CORS Configuration: Properly configured Cross - Origin Resource 
Sharing in Django REST Framework to control which domains can 
access the API 
API Throttling: Implemented rate limiting using Django REST 
Framework to prevent abuse and DDoS attacks 
Data Encryption: Sensitive data encrypted both in transit (HTTPS) 
and at rest in PostgreSQL 
Django Security Middleware: Utilizes Django's built -in security 
features including CSRF protection, XSS prevention, and SQL 
injection protection 
Input Validation: Comprehensive validation on both React frontend 
and Django backend sides 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 18. Json Web Token Authentication Process 
 
JWT authentication, or JSON Web Token authentication, is a system 
where a user logs in once using their username and password, and 
the server responds by giving a signed token. This token acts as 
proof of identity and must be sent with every request to acc ess 
protected features. Because the


===== PAGE 19 =====
token is digitally signed, it can’t be altered without being 
detected. 
 
JWT is important because your password is not repeatedly sent 
after login, reducing risk if traffic is intercepted. 
Tokens also expire automatically, limiting misuse. 
 
A JWT contains three parts: 
Header – the signing method 
Payload – user data and expiration time 
Signature – ensures the token is valid 
 
It provides fast, secure, and stateless authentication for web and 
mobile systems. 
 
Requirements Documentation 
Methods of Research 
This study employed a Descriptive  Development Research 
Methodology. Descriptive research involves gathering data that 
describes the nature and characteristics of a phenomenon or 
population, without influencing it [4]. The development aspect 
refers to the systematic process of designing, c reating, and 
evaluating a product to solve an identified problem. This method 
was chosen because it is ideally suited for capstone projects that 
aim to describe an existing problem (the inefficiencies in manual 
event management) and then develop a tangible  solution (the E-
Gate system) to address it. This approach allows for a structured 
investigation into the requirements and a methodical creation of 
the software product. 
 
Research Instruments 
 
To ensure comprehensive data collection, multiple research 
instruments were utilized. 
 
Interview 
An interview is a qualitative data collection method that involves 
direct, conversational questioning to gather in- depth information, 
opinions, and insights from individuals [4]. Interviews were 
conducted with Barangay officials to understand the intricac ies, 
challenges, and specific requirements of their current event 
management process. This helped the researchers identify core 
problems such as pass sharing and inaccurate headcounts, which 
directly informed the system's need for biometric verification and

Technical Background 
Core Technical Concepts 
 
QR Code Technology 
Quick Response (QR) codes are two -dimensional barcodes that 
can store significantly more information than traditional barcodes. 
For E-Gate, we utilize the HTML5 QR Code library to generate 
encrypted QR codes containing unique identifiers for each 
registered attendee. These codes are designed to be scanned 
quickly and securely, with encryption preventing easy replication 
or forgery. 
 
Facial Recognition System 
The facial recognition component uses the device's camera to 
capture and analyze facial features. The system creates a digital 
facial signature by mapping nodal points on a person's face and 
stores this encrypted data securely. 
During verification, it compares live captures against stored 
references to confirm identity. 
 
JWT Authentication 
We implement Simple JWT for secure authentication in our 
Django REST Framework. This provides digitally signed tokens 
that contain user information and permissions, ensuring each API 
request is independently verifiable without server -side session 
storage.

5. Resident Portal 
 
The Resident Portal lets residents manage their own event 
participation, verification, profile, QR code, and face enrollment. 
 
Sections: 
 
- Browse Events 
- My Registrations 
- Verify Account 
- Profile 
 
5.1 Browse Events 
 
Use Browse Events to find barangay events and register. 
 
Steps: 
 
1. Open Resident Portal. 
2. Select Browse Events. 
3. Use Active Events, Ended Events, or All Events. 
4. Search by event name if needed. 
5. Filter by status, event type, or ordering. 
6. Select Register for Event. 
 
Important: 
 
- Residents must be verified before registering for events. 
- If the account is not verified, select Go to Verification. 
- If already registered, the button changes to Unregister. 
- Ended events cannot be registered for. 
 
5.2 My Registrations 
 
Use My Registrations to review events the resident has joined. 
 
Typical use: 
 
- Check upcoming registered events. 
- Confirm event registration status. 
- Review previous registrations when available. 
 
5.3 Verify Account 
 
Use Verify Account to submit a Barangay ID or other required 
document for admin review. 
 
Steps: 
 
1. Open Resident Portal. 
2. Select Verify Account. 
3. Read the current verification status. 
4. Upload a clear JPG, PNG, WEBP, or PDF file. 
5. Add a note for the admin if needed. 
6. Select Submit for Verification. 
 
Status meanings: 
 
- Not Yet Verified: no approved verification is on file. 
- Pending Review: the request is waiting for admin review. 
- Verified: the account has been approved. 
- New Upload Requested: the admin rejected the previous upload 
and needs a clearer or updated document. 
 
Notes: 
 
- Only one pending request is allowed at a time. 
- Approved residents do not need to upload again unless an admin 
requests it. 
 
5.4 Profile 
 
Use Profile to view resident information and ID tools. 
 
Available actions: 
 
- View barangay ID. 
- Copy barangay ID. 
- View verification status. 
- View ID expiry. 
- Upload or update profile photo. 
- Download QR. 
- Print ID. 
- Enroll or re-enroll face recognition. 
 
Profile photo rules: 
 
- Accepted image types: JPG, PNG, WEBP. 
- Maximum file size: 5 MB. 
 
Face recognition: 
 
- Select Enroll Face or Re-enroll Face. 
- Allow camera permission. 
- Use good lighting and face the camera clearly. 
- Save the enrollment when capture succeeds. 
 
6. Gate Portal 
 
The Gate Portal supports operational scanning and logging at the 
gate. 
 
Modes: 
 
- Resident Log 
- Event Attendance 
- Guest Appointment 
 
The Gate Portal can show recent gate history, latest scan result, 
and time sheet views for resident and guest logs. 
 
6.1 Resident Log 
 
Use Resident Log for daily resident time in/time out tracking 
outside a specific event. 
 
Steps: 
 
1. Open Gate Portal. 
2. Select Resident Log. 
3. Choose Time In or Time Out. 
4. On a laptop or desktop, choose QR Scanner or Face Scanner. 
5. Scan the resident QR code or capture the resident face. 
6. Review the Latest Result panel. 
 
Notes: 
 
- Mobile devices can view logs, but scanner features are disabled 
for this mode. 
- Use a laptop or desktop gate station for resident QR and face 
scanning. 
- Recent logs update automatically. 
 
6.2 Event Attendance 
 
Use Event Attendance to mark attendance for a selected barangay

event. 
 
Steps: 
 
1. Open Gate Portal. 
2. Select Event Attendance. 
3. Select an active event. 
4. On a laptop or desktop, choose QR Scanner or Face Scanner. 
5. Scan the resident QR code or capture the resident face. 
6. Review the Latest Result panel. 
 
The system checks event registration, resident verification, and 
duplicate attendance rules. 
 
6.3 Guest Appointment 
 
Use Guest Appointment to process visitor check -in and check -
out. 
 
QR scanning steps: 
 
1. Open Gate Portal. 
2. Select Guest Appointment. 
3. On a laptop or desktop, choose QR Scanner. 
4. Scan the guest appointment QR. 
5. Review the result. 
 
Manual lookup steps: 
 
1. Open Gate Portal. 
2. Select Guest Appointment. 
3. Choose Manual Lookup. 
4. Search for the guest appointment. 
5. Record the guest check-in or check-out. 
 
Notes: 
 
- Mobile devices support Manual Lookup for guest processing. 
- Guest QR scanning is disabled on phones. 
- Cancelled or archived guest appointments cannot be used for 
normal scanning. 
- Time-out requires a prior time-in. 
 
6.4 Gate History and Time Sheets 
 
The Gate Portal includes a Gate History panel. 
 
Use it to: 
 
- Search resident, event, guest, purpose, or address depending on 
the active gate mode.

- Select an exact date for the time sheet. 
- Export CSV for resident or guest time sheets.

8. Common Messages and What To Do 
 
Account is not verified: 
 
- Resident should open Verify Account and submit a valid 
document. 
- Admin should review the request in Verifications. 
 
Event is full: 
 
- The event capacity has been reached. The resident cannot 
register unless capacity is increased. 
 
Registration is closed: 
 
- The event registration window has ended. Admin may edit the 
event window if needed. 
 
Resident already checked in: 
 
- Attendance has already been recorded. No extra action is needed 
unless there is a reporting issue. 
 
QR unavailable: 
 
- The appointment or profile may be inactive, archived, or missing 
required data. 
 
Camera not working: 
 
- Allow browser camera permission. 
- Close other apps using the camera. 
- Use a laptop or desktop for scanner modes that disable mobile 
scanning. 
- Refresh the page and reopen the scanner. 
 
Face recognition unavailable: 
 
- The backend may not have the optional face recognition 
environment installed. 
- Use QR scanning as the fallback. 
 
No analytics data: 
 
- Adjust the date range. 
- Confirm that attendance records exist for the selected period.

2. Use Resident Log for regular resident time in/time out. 
3. Use Event Attendance during barangay events. 
4. Use Guest Appointment for visitor check-in/check-out.

10. Safety and Privacy Reminders 
 
- Do not share administrator or gate operator accounts. 
- Log out after use, especially on shared devices. 
- Keep verification documents and resident records confidential. 
- Use Reveal Details only when needed for official work.

deleting them immediately.

consent according to barangay policy.
